7 Things to Check Before Buying a Used Car in India

A used car can be a great deal or a costly mistake — the difference usually comes down to what you check before paying. Here's what actually matters:

  1. Service history — a full, continuous service record tells you more than the odometer does. Gaps are a red flag.
  2. Accident history — ask for an inspection report, not just a visual check. Repainted panels and uneven gaps are easy to miss on your own.
  3. Odometer consistency — cross-check the reading against service stickers and past invoices. A tampered odometer is one of the most common used-car scams.
  4. RC and ownership papers — confirm the registration certificate matches the seller's ID, and check for any pending loan hypothecation on the RC.
  5. RTO and challan status — outstanding traffic challans or disputes transfer to you as the new owner if not cleared first.
  6. Insurance continuity — a lapsed policy usually means a higher premium and a fresh inspection requirement when you renew it.
  7. A real test drive — on a mix of city and highway roads, not just around the block. Listen for engine noise, check AC cooling, and test the brakes properly.

How We Actually Calculate Your Car's Resale Value

Most instant valuation tools quietly either lowball sellers or overpromise and disappoint them at inspection. Here's how ours is actually built:

We start from a real ex-showroom reference price for your exact brand, model and variant — not a generic brand average. From there, a depreciation curve calibrated against real market listings (not a flat percentage) accounts for age, and that curve is different for hatchbacks, mid-size sedans/SUVs, and luxury cars, because they genuinely depreciate at different rates in the Indian market.

On top of that, your car's actual kilometres driven, number of owners, fuel type and city all adjust the number — a low-mileage, single-owner car is worth more than an identical one with heavy use and multiple owners, and our formula reflects that instead of treating every car of the same model as identical.

The estimate is a starting point, confirmed after a free physical inspection — but it's built to be close from the first number you see, not a teaser that falls apart in person.

Car Insurance Renewal: What Most Owners Get Wrong

A surprising number of insurance claims get delayed or rejected for reasons that have nothing to do with the accident itself. The most common ones:

  • Letting the policy lapse — even a short gap can mean a mandatory fresh inspection and a higher premium on renewal, sometimes with exclusions for pre-existing wear.
  • Under-declaring modifications — any change from the manufacturer's spec (alloy wheels, CNG kits, etc.) needs to be declared, or a related claim can be denied.
  • Ignoring the IDV (Insured Declared Value) — accepting whatever IDV the insurer defaults to, instead of negotiating it, directly shrinks your payout in a total-loss claim.
  • No-claim bonus left unclaimed — this follows you, not the car. If you switch insurers or cars, make sure it's carried over — it's often worth a meaningful discount.
